## What Curbio is

Curbio is the pre-listing home improvement partner for real estate agents — a
licensed general contractor that gets a listing market-ready on time and on
budget, sold **to and through agents**, with pay-at-closing financing for
qualified sellers.

Curbio is singular: *Curbio does the work*, never *Curbio do*.

---

## The audiences, and what each one actually cares about

Three audiences, three different anxieties. The product is the same; the
argument is not.

### Listing agents — the primary buyer

Agents are the channel and the customer. They recommend Curbio to their seller
clients. What they care about:

- **Winning the listing.** A concierge offering is a competitive weapon in the
  listing appointment — the Compass Concierge benchmark is the reference point
  agents already understand.
- **Their reputation.** They are recommending a contractor to a client. A
  project that runs late or over budget lands on the agent, not on Curbio.
  Fast starts and on-time completion are reputation protection, not features.
- **Not doing the work.** One call, one project manager, one timeline. Agents
  do not want to coordinate trades.
- **Their own economics.** More listings won, faster days-on-market, higher
  sale price.

Tone with agents: partner-to-partner, outcome-focused. **Never talk past or
above the agent.** The agent is the trusted local advisor; Curbio simply steps
in as the contractor.

### Brokerages and teams — the enterprise motion

One relationship, one decision-maker, a book of agents behind them. What they
care about:

- **Recruit and retain.** A concierge program is a reason for an agent to join
  and a reason to stay.
- **Competing with Compass** without carrying the capital risk themselves.
- **White-label or co-brand.** They can run it as their own "Concierge"
  program, or as Curbio.

Tone: strategic, credible. This is a business-case conversation, not a
brochure.

### Home sellers — the end user, reached through the agent

Sellers rarely choose Curbio directly; their agent recommends it. What they
care about:

- **Not managing the work themselves.** This is the whole promise.
- **Not paying up front.** Pay-at-closing is the objection-killer.
- **Today's buyers want move-in ready homes** — the market reason to do the
  work at all.

Tone: warm, calm, simple. No contractor jargon.

## The pay-at-closing proposition — approved phrasing

Use this phrasing. Do not paraphrase it into something stronger.

> **Pay at closing.** Qualified sellers pay nothing up front — Curbio is paid
> from the proceeds when the home sells.

Two qualifiers are load-bearing and must survive every rewrite:

1. **"Qualified"** — pay-at-closing is available to *qualifying* customers, not
   to everyone. Dropping the qualifier turns an offer into a promise.
2. **"When the home sells"** — payment is at closing, tied to the sale.

Never write pay-at-closing as "free," "no cost," "we only get paid if you
sell more," or anything implying Curbio carries the risk if the home does not
sell. Fine print on any surface that quantifies an outcome: *individual results
may vary; qualifying customers only.*

### Claims that need review — do not publish without sign-off

Curbio has regulatory history (a DC Attorney General matter) attached to
quantified seller-outcome claims. The 2026 strategy is explicit: reclaim the
three proven value propositions — pay-at-closing, sell faster and for more, and
the concierge solution for agents — **while avoiding the specific unfounded
claims that caused the trouble.** Practically:

- **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW —** any specific dollar figure a seller will gain
  ("sell for $50k more"), any specific percentage of price lift, any specific
  days-on-market reduction, stated as a promise rather than as an observed
  result of a named project.
- **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW —** "sell faster and for more" when stated without
  qualification. The *proposition* is approved; an unqualified quantified
  version of it is not.
- **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW —** guaranteed-profit or guaranteed-ROI framing in any
  form ("guaranteed profit," "double your commission," "you'll make it back").
- **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW —** anything a creator or affiliate says on Curbio's
  behalf. The strategy requires a written compliance guardrail listing approved
  and prohibited claims *before* any creator dollar goes out; an unmonitored
  creator promising an outcome is a liability multiplier.

When asked to draft copy containing any of the above, produce the draft and
flag the specific line as needing legal review. Do not silently soften it and
do not silently include it.

## Voice and tone

Curbio sounds like **the dependable pro who has done this thousands of times
and makes it easy.** Trusted, expert, reassuring, refreshingly plain.

- **Reassuring.** We sell peace of mind. *Hassle-free, stress-free, simple, on
  time, on budget, taken care of, list with confidence.*
- **Expert and confident.** Licensed and insured. "We cross the finish line
  with you." Confident — never boastful.
- **Agent-protective.** The agent is the trusted local advisor. Curbio steps in
  as the contractor. Never undercut the agent's authority.
- **Clear.** Short sentences. Concrete benefits. No contractor jargon, no hype.

### Person and CTAs

- **First person plural** internally: *we, our team, we'll.* Never *I*.
- **Second person** to the reader: *you, your listings, your clients.*
- **CTAs are short and active:** *Request a free estimate. · Ask your agent. ·
  Get started.* Never "click here," never "learn more."

### Verbatim brand lines — reuse these, don't reinvent

- "The pre-listing home improvement experts."
- "The one call that does it all."
- "From start to finish."
- "On time and on budget."
- "List with confidence. We'll take care of the rest."
- "Fast starts, on-time completion, and expert service."
- "Today's buyers want move-in ready homes."
- "Get your home market-ready — without managing the work yourself."
- "One team. One timeline. One accountable partner."
- "Update before you list."

### The headline pattern

Serif headline in navy with **one or two amber emphasis words** — the emphasis
is *color only*, same weight, upright, never italic. Pick the verb or the value
word.

> Today's buyers want **move-in ready** homes.
> One team. One timeline. One **accountable** partner.

Never make a whole headline amber. If everything is amber, nothing is.

## Casing and micro-style — non-negotiable

- **`pre-listing`** is hyphenated. Every time.
- **`REALTOR®`** always carries the registered mark.
- **`Curbio`** in body text; lowercase `curbio` only in the wordmark. Singular.
- **Sentence case** for headlines. All-caps only for eyebrow labels
  (`DEDICATED PROJECT MANAGEMENT`, `BEFORE`, `AFTER`).
- **No emoji.** Not in any collateral. Not even one.
- **No exclamation points** in headlines or body. Calm.

---

## Terms and moves to avoid

**Language:**

- Pushy sales language, fear-mongering, exclamation overload, buzzwords.
- Over-promises: "guaranteed profit," "double your commission," "you can't
  lose."
- Anything that undercuts the agent's authority or speaks past them to the
  seller.
- "Free" or "no cost" applied to pay-at-closing.
- Contractor jargon and construction-trade shorthand.
- "Click here," "learn more."

**Structural:**

- Never write `pre listing` or `prelisting`.
- Never drop the ® from REALTOR.
- Never state the Compass Concierge benchmark as a Curbio result.
- Never quantify a seller outcome without the fine-print qualifier and a legal
  flag.
