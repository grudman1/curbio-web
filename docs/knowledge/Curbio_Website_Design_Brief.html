02 — Design principles for web

Six rules that decide every layout.

When a screen feels noisy, dated, cheap, or salesy — start over. If it feels sophisticated, airy, modern, and trusted, you're on brand.

1

Photography is the hero, not chrome.

Real homes, beautifully lit, high-key. Full-bleed photography is the one approved "background." Lead heroes and section breaks with a real interior or before/after pair — never stock collages, gradients, or illustration.

2

Let it breathe.

Wide margins, generous gaps, one idea per block. Sections are 64–96px apart; hero whitespace is luxurious. When in doubt, add 16px. Density is the enemy of "high-end."

3

Navy carries the type. Amber is a spark.

Most text on light backgrounds is navy, not black. Amber appears only on CTAs, one or two emphasis words, AFTER tags, and stars. If everything is amber, nothing is.

4

Calm motion only.

Fade + 8px rise on entrance, 220ms. Hover changes color and shadow — never scale, skew, or bounce. The brand is reassuring; the interface should feel settled.

5

Respect the agent.

Even homeowner-facing pages frame Curbio as the contractor the agent brings in. Never talk past or above the agent's authority.

6

Paper-real, not web 3.0.

No mesh gradients, glassmorphism, grain, neon, or glow. Flat solid navy panels, clean whites, restrained navy-tinted shadows. The web should feel like good print.

03 — Color

A navy + amber system on a Cloud White canvas.

Exact values — do not approximate. Saturation stays low; the page breathes on near-whites and stone.

Amber is reserved for accents and large display words — never small body text on white (it fails contrast and cheapens the spark). Web text and interactive elements must meet WCAG AA: 4.5:1 for body, 3:1 for large headings and UI.

Lora for voice. Libre Franklin for clarity.

The signature headline move

Serif headline in navy, with one or two amber emphasis words — pick the verb or the value word. The emphasis is color only: same weight, upright, no italic. Use <em> inside any headline and the system colors it amber automatically.

05 — Spacing, layout & grid

A 4px scale and a centered editorial column.

Reach for 24–48px between sub-sections, 64–96px between major sections and for hero whitespace.

Container & breakpoints

Content lives in a centered column with a max-width of ~1180px and a minimum 32px gutter. Full-bleed photography breaks out edge-to-edge; everything else stays inside the column.

Desktop≥ 1024px — multi-column grids, side-by-side before/after
Tablet640–1023px — 2-up grids, nav collapses at 880px
Mobile< 640px — single column, stacked, 24px gutter

Radii & elevation

Cards use --r-lg (12px) + --shadow-md. Hero panels and soft cards use --r-xl (20px). Buttons and tags are always pills (999px). Shadows are restrained and navy-tinted — never blown-out gray, never inner shadow or glow.

06 — Components

The frontend kit.

Live specimens, built on the brand tokens. These are the building blocks every page composes from.

Buttons

Primary is a solid amber pill — one per view, the single most important action. Secondary is a navy outline that fills on hover. Tertiary is an underlined navy link. Hover deepens color and adds shadow; never scale. CTAs are short and active — Request a free estimate · Ask your agent · Get started. No "click here," no "learn more" as the primary.

07 — Page architecture

How a Curbio page is assembled.

Every page is a vertical stack of full-width sections in a consistent rhythm. Photography and navy panels alternate with airy Cloud White content blocks.

Standard page stack

  1. Sticky navy nav — wordmark, links, one amber CTA.
  2. Hero — full-bleed photo, serif headline with amber emphasis, short amber rule, sub, primary CTA.
  3. Value row — 3 icon-disc features ("the one call that does it all").
  4. Before/After proof — real project pairing.
  5. How it works — numbered steps, one idea each.
  6. Comparison — Curbio vs. traditional (navy + amber table header).
  7. Testimonial — stone quote card + stars.
  8. Navy CTA closer — headline + amber pill + estimate form.
  9. Footer bar — white wordmark + tagline.

08 — Voice & copy

Trusted, expert, reassuring, refreshingly plain.

We sound like the dependable pro who has done this thousands of times and makes it easy. Confident, never boastful. Reuse the verbatim brand lines before writing fresh marketing copy.

Verbatim lines — reuse these

  • "The one call that does it all."
  • "On time and on budget."
  • "List with confidence. We'll take care of the rest."
  • "Today's buyers want move-in ready homes."
  • "One team. One timeline. One accountable partner."

Micro-style rules

  • pre-listing is always hyphenated.
  • REALTOR® always carries the mark.
  • Curbio is singular — "Curbio does the work."
  • Sentence case headlines; ALL-CAPS only for eyebrows.
  • Second person to the reader ("your listings"), first-person plural for us ("we'll").
  • No emoji. No exclamation points in headlines or body.
09 — Do & don't

The quick gut-check before shipping a screen.

Do

  • Lead with real, high-key home photography.
  • Keep navy as the ink, amber as a single spark.
  • Use the serif headline + one or two amber emphasis words.
  • Give every section room — 64–96px of air.
  • Make buttons amber pills; one primary per view.
  • Pair before/after with navy + amber tags.
  • Meet WCAG AA contrast on all text and controls.
  • Frame Curbio as the contractor the agent brings in.

Don't

  • Use Lora italic, or italicize emphasis words.
  • Put amber on body text, backgrounds, or large blocks.
  • Use teal on text or anywhere in the logo.
  • Add gradients, mesh, glassmorphism, grain, or glow.
  • Scale, bounce, or spring on hover.
  • Use emoji, unicode arrows, or hand-drawn SVG.
  • Crowd the layout or stack two ideas in one block.
  • Use exclamation points or pushy sales language.
